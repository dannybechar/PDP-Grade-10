# B1.3 — Frame-Based Motion

**Strategy document** | Unit B: Creative Programming | B1: The Draw Loop & Creative Environment

## Position and scope

B1.3 uses three 45-minute lessons to move from one remembered value to coordinated change:

1. **B1.3.1 — Frame-by-Frame Change**
2. **B1.3.2 — Velocity-Style Movement**
3. **B1.3.3 — Combining & Practicing**

The sequence introduces cumulative change without conditionals. Shapes may leave the canvas;
bouncing and wrapping are intentionally deferred until rules can be expressed with conditions.

## Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

- Distinguish a local variable initialized inside `draw()` from a persistent sketch variable
  declared outside all methods.
- Explain why an increment accumulates only when the updated value is remembered between frames.
- Change one property by a fixed amount per frame.
- Move a shape with `position = position + speed`.
- Relate the sign of x/y increments to screen direction.
- Combine two changing properties and predict their joint visible effect.

## Technical baseline

- A variable declared and initialized inside `draw()` receives that initializer on every call.
- A class-level field can be read and updated by `draw()` across frames.
- `background()` near the start of `draw()` clears previous pixels when a clean moving frame is
  intended; omitting it deliberately produces a trail.
- Positive x moves right, negative x left; positive y moves down, negative y up.
- Motion is measured in units per frame, not units per second.
- `float` supports fractional movement but is not mandatory for whole-number steps.
- Boundary checks, transformations, multiple independently tracked elements, and unfamiliar-code
  tracing remain outside B1.3.

## Lesson progression

### Remembered change

Two near-identical snippets contrast a local size value with a persistent size field. Students
trace several frames, explain why one appears unchanged, and build a growing-circle sketch.

### Velocity-style movement

A named position and speed make `x = x + speed` readable and testable. Students calculate a few
frames by hand, compare positive and negative movement on both axes, then construct a steady
one-direction motion sketch.

### Combination

Students add a second changing property—such as size, y-position, or grayscale intensity—to a
known motion sketch. The task consolidates independent state updates without introducing a new
mechanism.

## Misconceptions to surface

- Repeating `d = d + 1` does not accumulate if `d` is reinitialized inside every `draw()` call.
- Positive y does not mean upward movement on screen.
- A target frame rate is not an exact clock.
- A trail is retained pixel history, not several independently stored shapes.

## Assessment evidence

Students classify variable scope, trace numerical values across frames, predict direction and
position, and construct a sketch with at least two deliberate frame-by-frame changes.

## Implementation status

Implemented in `05-frame-by-frame-change` through `07-combining-practicing`. The three revised
presentations maintain the local-versus-persistent progression and the frame-based prediction
model. All eighteen supporting BlueJ projects compile independently against Processing 4.5.6.
